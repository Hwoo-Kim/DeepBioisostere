DeepBioisostere

Autonomous Bioisosteric Replacement for Multi-Property Optimization in Drug Design

Hyeongwoo Kim1,*, Seokhyun Moon1,*, Wonho Zhung1, Shinwoo Kim1, Jaechang Lim2, Woo Youn Kim1,2,†
1KAIST 2HITS Inc.
*Equal contribution  ·  †Corresponding author
Nature Communications 2026
Paper Code Data PyPI

DeepBioisostere proposes replacement fragments that preserve biological activity while moving several molecular properties toward their targets — including bioisosteres that do not appear in the training data.

Abstract

Optimizing molecular properties while preserving biological activity is a central challenge in drug design. Bioisosteric replacement, which substitutes a molecular fragment with a chemically or biologically analogous moiety, offers a powerful strategy for fine-tuning properties without disrupting target binding. However, existing in silico approaches often rely on expert-defined modification sites or struggle to modulate multiple molecular properties simultaneously.

Here, we present DeepBioisostere, a deep generative model that performs end-to-end bioisosteric replacement by autonomously selecting and substituting molecular fragments to satisfy multiple target properties. The model captures complex relationships across the molecular graph, enabling the optimization of sophisticated properties such as drug-likeness and synthetic accessibility. By learning from experimental bioassay data, DeepBioisostere proposes replacements that maintain biological activities, even generating potential bioisosteres beyond the training data. We demonstrate the effectiveness of the model in computational hit-to-lead optimization scenarios, highlighting its potential to accelerate rational molecular design without relying on expert heuristics or pre-established substitution rules.

What the model does

Chooses where to edit

No expert-defined modification site is required. The model scores every BRICS-decomposable subgraph of the input molecule and decides which fragment to remove.

Optimizes several properties at once

Conditioning is on the change in each property, so multiple objectives — molecular weight, logP, QED, synthetic accessibility — can be steered jointly rather than one at a time.

Goes beyond known rules

Trained on matched molecular pairs from experimental bioassay data, it proposes replacements outside any pre-established substitution table.

Hit-to-lead case study

Applied to structure-based generative model outputs, DeepBioisostere improves drug-likeness and synthetic accessibility while retaining the predicted binding mode.

Get started

Install from PyPI — no conda environment and no compiled PyG extensions required:

pip install deepbioisostere

Optimize a molecule for two properties at once:

deepbioisostere generate \
  --smiles "Cc1ccc2cnc(N(C)CCc3ccccn3)nc2c1" \
  --target mw=0 --target logp=-1 \
  --output results.csv

Or from Python:

from deepbioisostere import Conditioner, DeepBioisostere, Generator

properties = ["logp", "mw"]
model = DeepBioisostere.from_pretrained(properties=properties)
generator = Generator(
    model=model,
    conditioner=Conditioner(phase="generation", properties=properties),
    properties=properties,
)
df = generator.generate([("Cc1ccc2cnc(N(C)CCc3ccccn3)nc2c1", {"mw": 0, "logp": -1})])

Model weights and the fragment library are downloaded and cached on first use.
